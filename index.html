How I Work with AI

More done with less. Every rule here came out of something I built.

The model explains. It doesn't make the numbers.

In FPL Buddy a projection engine decides every pick. The language model only puts the engine's stored output into plain words. There's deliberately no chat box.

FPL Buddy, architecture decision 0004

Beat the simple baseline, or don't ship.

Before a friend saw a single recommendation, the engine had to beat "average of the last five gameweeks" on a replay of the whole 2025/26 season. Its captain picks averaged 6.08 points against the baseline's 4.87.

FPL Buddy, requirement FR-1

Count the tokens.

Productivity and efficiency go together. FPL Buddy's language model runs on a £10-a-month budget, and Project Architect keeps day-one docs to around 300 lines so the model's context goes on real work, not guesses.

FPL Buddy LLM budget; Project Architect context budget

If a formula will do, don't use a model.

The values quiz scores people with a published formula that runs in their browser. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. AI helps draft questions at build time, and people review them.

Values quiz, rules 1 and 2

AI review is a first pass. People sign off.

AI reviewers catch plenty early on, but they never count as approval. The quiz waits for real reviewers from different parties, faiths and countries.

Values quiz, rule 6

Keep score on your own advice.

Every FPL Buddy recommendation is stored with what happened next, including the weeks it said "save your transfer", so the product can't sound surer than its track record.

FPL Buddy, requirements FR-4 and R4

My Journey

The Spark: Engineering & Ideation

My path into product began not in a business school, but during my engineering studies at NIT Trichy, fascinated by data, technology, and participating in ideation contests. This was my first taste of how technology could be shaped to solve interesting problems.

The Foundation: Building at Scale

This curiosity led me to Citi, where I spent three years as a software developer. I moved from theory to practice, building an ML model that prevented over 100k trade failures (saving an estimated $8M+ annually) and developing data visualization tools for capital markets operations. This experience taught me how to translate complex technical solutions into tangible business impact.

The Bridge: From "How" to "Why"

While I loved the technical challenge, I was driven to get closer to the "why." This led me to an MBA at IIM Bangalore. There, I honed my strategic thinking in national competitions, placing 2nd runners-up nationally in HUL LIME — 2 of 20,000+ teams — with a gamified marketing strategy. I learned to build the strategic bridge between code and customer.

The Accelerator: Startup Agility

While pursuing my MBA, I worked part-time at the AI startup RagaAI, juggling academic work with real-world product challenges. I learned to operate with speed and agility—conducting market research, mapping customer journeys, and collaborating on design in a fast-paced environment.

The Synthesis: Enterprise Impact

Today I'm a product manager at JPMorgan Chase in London and the firm's SME for UK Personal Loans — I own the requirements, the test strategy and the payment flows, and I've watched the thing go from a blank page to its first live release. Before that I helped get the Chase UK credit card through its customer-panel phase.

Somewhere in there I started building again. Nobody asked me to. I wanted to know whether these models could do the careful, boring parts of the job — reading regulation, checking that a requirement actually says what it's meant to say — so I built the tools and found out. More and more people use them now, and I'm working on turning them into official use cases and tools for users. One placed 3rd in our line of business at the firm's AI hackathon, and an Operations team uses a loan simulator I built to settle customer disputes. The part I care about most is making them cheap enough to keep running.

Sustainability
