I work out where AI belongs in a product. And where it doesn't.

Product manager at JPMorgan Chase in London, and the firm's SME for UK Personal Loans. Outside work I build AI products end to end, because finding out what a model can actually do is quicker than arguing about it.

Where I think this is heading: everyone has access to the same models now, so the model isn't the edge. The edge is the unglamorous stuff around it. What it's allowed to decide, how you know it got it right, and whether it's cheap enough to keep running.

How I build with AI

Rules I've ended up with. Each one came out of a real project, noted on the right.

The model explains the answer. It doesn't make the numbers.

In FPL Buddy a projection engine decides every transfer and captain pick. The language model gets the engine's stored output and puts it into plain words, and that's all it's allowed to do. There's deliberately no chat box.

FPL Buddy, architecture decision 0004

Beat the dumb baseline, or don't ship.

Before a friend saw a single recommendation, the engine had to beat "average of the last five gameweeks" on a replay of the whole 2025/26 season, using only what was known before each deadline. Its captain picks averaged 6.08 points against the baseline's 4.87. The first engine I built got thrown out on the way there.

FPL Buddy, requirement FR-1

If a formula will do the job, don't use a model.

The values quiz scores people with a published, deterministic formula that runs in their browser. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. AI helps draft the questions at build time, and people review them. When someone's politics is on the line, "the model thought so" isn't a good enough answer.

Values quiz, rules 1 and 2

AI review is a first pass. People sign off.

I use AI reviewers to catch problems early, and they catch plenty. They never count as approval. The quiz doesn't launch until real people from different parties, faiths and countries have read every question.

Values quiz, rule 6

Keep score on your own advice.

Every FPL Buddy recommendation gets stored along with what happened next, including the weeks it told you to save your transfer. The confidence label gets built from that record ("moves like this worked 6 in 10"), so the product can't sound surer than its track record.

FPL Buddy

Closed beta with friends

Fantasy Premier League advice for your actual squad, with an honest sense of how sure it is. Enter your public Team ID and that's it. It never asks for your FPL login.

The bet
FPL tools hand you data. What managers actually want is a decision, and quite often the right one is "do nothing this week". Using it should mean showing up before deadlines, not spending more time in the app.
The AI call
A point-source projection engine in Python makes every number. The language model only writes short summaries from that engine's stored output. Each engine change is replayed against last season before it goes live.
Where it stands
Live since Gameweek 6, rolling out to more friends from Gameweek 11. Runs on Cloud Run, Supabase and Vercel, built to cost close to £0 at friends scale.
  • 965 commits
  • 1,800+ tests
  • 14 architecture decisions

Values quiz

Waiting on human review

A quiz that shows people, mostly the Indian diaspora, what they actually value, separate from which party they support.

The bet
Party loyalty hides what people actually believe. Anyone, from any party or religion, should be just as likely to learn something uncomfortable about themselves. No position gets framed as the smart one.
The AI call
No AI at runtime and no storage at all. Every placement is backed by a free, citable source, and if the source doesn't say, the cell says silent rather than guessing.
Where it stands
The scoring engine and screens are built. It's waiting for real reviewers across parties before it launches.

  • 0 bytes stored

Project Architect

Public plugin

A Claude Code plugin that interviews you about a project idea, makes the architecture calls, and sets up the repo with lean docs and session-safety rules.

The bet
On day one you know the least you'll ever know about your project. Long upfront docs are guesses, and they eat the model's context in every session after. So it records decisions and constraints, and lets the rest grow from real code.
Where it came from
More than 40 sessions building a full-stack AI travel app. Each doc it generates is there because something went wrong without it.

Paused, shipped, learned from

Knowing when to stop counts as a product decision too.

  • OverlapFootball draft game, paused

    Draft an all-time XI where the score comes from chemistry between players who actually played together, not the sum of their ratings. The market leader has 5.4M players and a leaderboard full of identical maximum scores, because every pick has an obvious right answer. The design and the season simulation are calibrated against real football. It's paused before the build.

  • Vibe TravelPaused

    Turn Instagram travel reels into itineraries you can book. I did the research before building: Expedia already turns Reels into itineraries with Trip Matching, and Instagram's API no longer supports personal accounts. So any version of this needs a much narrower angle than "paste a reel".

  • AI Itinerary CrafterLive

    For long or multi-city trips it plans the route first, in about five seconds, and then fills in each day. The route and the detail are two separate jobs, so they get two separate passes.

  • UK Loan VisualizerLive

    Overpay your loan or invest the money? It compares the two over the same timeframe and works out the return you'd need to break even.
